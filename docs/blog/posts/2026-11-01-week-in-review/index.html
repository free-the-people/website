Trump suing Alaska: But only if his preferred candidate loses the race [Link]

Donny 2-Dolls

Donny 2-Dolls ordered the DOJ to block certification of the Alaska U.S. Senate election results unless his preferred candidate wins the race. The administration claims that this move will help ensure election integrity because both candidates for the state's Senate seat have similar names: Republican Dan Sullivan and independent candidate Daniel J. Sullivan.

This is a blatant attempt by the federal government to meddle in state electoral processes. Both names have been on the final ballot since it was set months ago, and the state's Supreme Court has already weighed in on the issue, saying there is nothing wrong with both candidates running.

The Trump regime is claiming that having both names on the ballot is an attempt to trick voters into voting for the wrong candidate. But the regime is saying that the election results should only be invalidated if the Republican Dan Sullivan loses. But if the problem is voter confusion, why not invalidate the results either way? If Republican voters can be fooled into voting for Daniel J Sullivan, why is it less likely that independent or Democratic voters might be fooled into voting for Dan Sullivan? If the problem is voter confusion, neither outcome would be valid. If the problem is corruption, then only the regime's unwanted outcome is considered invalid.

What happens after the polls close? [Link]

Image by Ray_Shrewsberry from Pixabay.

The midterm elections are open for early voting in most states, but the final day of voting will be Tuesday, November 3. It may take a few days for all of the votes to be officially counted in every race. But even when the votes are all tallied, the new Members of Congress will not take office until January 3, 2027. Until then, Congress is in what is known as a "lame duck" session, as MOCs who were not re-elected have no incentive to act on any legislation in this period, and this is often a period where little is accomplished.

Congress will have to pass some sort of spending bill to fund the government past December 11, when current funding runs out. What this battle looks like will probably depend on how the election turned out. If Democrats are going to flip one or both chambers of Congress, the Republicans might make a last ditch effort to try to pass funding for their pet projects before they lose the majority. But most likely, there will be a lot of arguments and in the end, Congress will approve a spending bill that kicks the can down the road to mid- or late-January.

If Republicans don't do well in the election, we can expect the Trump regime to try to overturn the election results. They have already begun the process by filing a suit to prematurely and conditionally reject the results of Alaska's senate rate. They will continue to lie about the scale of illegal voting by non-citizens, despite the fact that this has repeatedly been shown to be a minor problem that does not affect the outcome of elections.
