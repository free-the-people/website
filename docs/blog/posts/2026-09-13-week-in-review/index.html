Supreme Court blocks Missouri gerrymandered maps [Link]

Missouri's Secretary of State Denny Hoskins ordered local election authorities to use new district maps favoring Republicans despite orders from a federal judge telling him to hold off on the new maps. In an emergency hearing this week, the Supreme Court weighed in on the issue and ordered Missouri to revert to their previous district maps for the midterm elections. The Missouri Supreme Court found Hoskins in contempt for using the new maps in the primaries, but chose not to impose any penalties.

Aside from the blatant immorality Hoskins demonstrated, he has also done significant damage to the midterm elections in general. The new district maps were used for the primaries that have now concluded, but the state will have to revert back to the previous maps for the general election in November. Candidates who won the primaries in August have been certified for the ballot in the district they won, but that district may no longer hold the same constituents in November.

Missouri was the second state to rush for the opportunity to kiss their king's boots when Trump asked for states to find him more Republican seats in Congress last year. Hundreds of thousands of voters in Missouri signed a petition to ask for the state to vote on whether or not to adopt new district maps. The signatures were submitted in December, but Hoskins ignored them until the day of the primaries when he rejected the voters' request. The Missouri Supreme Court ruled that the redistricting referendum required voter approval, and the issue is now on the November ballot and the state is reverting to its original district maps. The state appealed to the U.S. Supreme Court to block the state court ruling, but the Supreme Court refused the request.

Russell Vought lets companies get away with exploiting Americans [Link]

“Impeach Russell Vought” by Joe Flood, CC BY-NC 4.0.

Russell Vought leads the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), which is responsible for protecting consumers from unfair, deceptive, or abusive practices by financial institutions. But it turns out that it's hard to hold misbehaving financial institutions accountable for their actions, so...he won't.

Vought has already tried to dismantle the CFPB, ordered mass layoffs of its employees, and desperately tried to choke off its funding. His attempts have completely failed because the CFPB was created by and is funded by Congress, and only Congress can shut it down.

Vought has claimed that this approach has been successful by pointing to a startup company called Bilt that offers credit cards specifically used to make rent and mortgage payments. The company failed to protect its customers during a critical transition that led to significant financial hardship for its customers. Instead of sending CFPB examiners to ensure that customers got relief for their hardships, Vought's CFPB trusted the company's promise that they had fixed the issues. Just a few weeks later, Bilt again failed to follow regulations, leading to additional harm to their customers that caused their credit scores to go down (Which can have cascading consequences, as credit scores are used to determine lending rates, insurance rates, and more).

The issues with Bilt are merely one example of the underlying problem. Having completely failed in his attempts to shut down the CFPB, Vought has resorted to undermining everything the bureau does. He ordered the bureau to shift its focus to deregulation (Just getting rid of the laws that corporations are expected to follow) and to just trust companies to fix any problems the bureau might find without facing any consequences or follow-up investigations. If he can't get rid of the CFPB, he's determined to render it completely ineffective, which still leads to the same result: American consumers get screwed over and the U.S. government does nothing to protect them.

Judges in some areas deliberately delay habeas corpus cases [Link]

Image by Peter H from Pixabay.

When immigrants are detained unlawfully by ICE agents, their only recourse is to file a habeas corpus petition, asking for a judge to determine whether their detention is lawful. With the recent surge in ICE depredations in our communities, the number of detainees filing habeas petitions has increased significantly, but an investigation by ProPublica found that certain judges have been taking significantly longer to rule on these cases than others.

The longest wait times have been in Mississippi and Louisiana, two deep red states. Of course, wait times have been growing longer for all immigration cases as the system is becoming overwhelmed by the sheer volume of cases it's being presented with. Our immigration courts were already overworked and understaffed before the Trump regime launched its aggressive campaign against immigrants. But for at least half of the cases resolved in Mississippi have taken 92 days or longer. This is not the immigration case itself, to determine whether the immigrant has a legal right to remain in the United States. This is just the hearing to determine if the detention of that individual is lawful. That means these migrants are spending months in prison before being told whether their imprisonment is even legal. Conditions in most of these detention facilities are appalling, if not downright inhumane.

Some states have done better than others in handling the surge in habeas petitions: Minnesota has a median close time of 12 days. That's still a long time to spend in prison waiting to see if you should even be there, but it's a lot more reasonable than 92 days.
