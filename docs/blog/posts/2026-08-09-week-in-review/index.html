Blanche agrees to kill $1.8 billion January 6 compensation fund in exchange for getting his nomination for Attorney General through committee [Link]

Images from January 6, 2021, insurrection, including the noose that was raised with the intention of hanging Vice President Mike Pence. Image by Aca1291 from Wikimedia Commons. Licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution-Share Alike 4.0 International license.

In January of this year, Donny 2-Dolls filed a lawsuit against his own Department of Treasury alleging the IRS leaked his 2019-2020 tax returns to the press. The DOJ settled the case out of court and, instead of pursuing the case, agreed to create a $1.8 billion fund to provide payments to January 6 insurrectionists who claimed they were unfairly prosecuted. In addition, the settlement included a provision that the Trump family and businesses could never be audited by the IRS for past or future inquiries.

Todd Blanche was nominated to replace Pam Bondi as Attorney General of the United States, but his confirmation has been held up because not all Republicans have been willing to support his nomination. This week, the last two Republican holdouts agreed to support Blanche's nomination in exchange for a written guarantee that Blanche would rescind the $1.8 billion "Anti-Weaponization Fund" that allowed January 6 insurrectionists to claim payments from the federal government for their ensuing prosecution.

In addition to written promises to cancel the January 6 fund, Blanche also agreed (in writing) that the provision exempting Trump and his family and businesses from audits would only apply to audits open at the time of the settlement.

Unfortunately, now that Blanche's nomination has been confirmed, he is on his way to officially becoming the Attorney General, where he will undoubtedly serve as Trump's lapdog and continue to use the Department of Justice to target and punish Trump's political enemies with blatant disregard for the Constitution and rule of law.

Jeanine Pirro admits Reflecting Pool damage was the result of shoddy construction [Link]

Painting the Reflecting Pool blue. Image by G. Edward Johnson from Wikimedia Commons. Licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution-Share Alike 4.0 International license.

For months now, Donny 2-Dolls has been pushing the false claim that the Reflecting Pool was damaged by vandals. U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia Jeanine Pirro filed charges against David Hearn, a former Olympian, who was seen touching the damaged Reflecting Pool liner and later accused of damaging the national landmark. It was obvious to everyone who saw the problems with the Reflecting Pool that the damage was far too extensive and pervasive to be attributed to vandalism, but the Trump regime doubled down on its accusations, and Pirro publicly announced that Hearn was guilty before the trial.

Less than a month later, the U.S. Attorney's office was forced to drop the charges against Hearn, admitting they had no evidence of vandalism and that the damage to the Reflecting Pool was the result of shoddy installation that cost taxpayers more than $15 million and left the monument in worse shape than it was before Trump decided it needed to be "fixed."

Naturally, Donny 2-Dolls is furious, claiming Pirro "folded like an umbrella," and it is likely that Pirro will be fired. She won't be fired for filing false charges against an innocent man, but for backing down and admitting that there was not enough evidence to proceed with the blatantly unlawful case.

ICE policy is to withhold body cam footage unless it shows agents in a favorable light [Link]

Image by Dyan Bone from Wikimedia Commons.

In exchange for continued funding, ICE has recently been required to equip officers with body cameras. This requirement comes in response to multiple murders committed by ICE agents and the tendency of the agents and the department to lie about the circumstances surrounding those murders.

The cameras, paid for by taxpayers, may be of limited use, considering that ICE's policy is to only release footage from these cameras if doing so is in the "best interests of the agency." The decision on whether to release footage will be made solely at the discretion of the director of ICE, and the policy does not specify what circumstances warrant withholding the footage.

When Alex Pretti was murdered by CBP agents (A sister agency to ICE), his death was captured by body cameras worn by agents, but CBP has still not released footage of the killing. There is no reason to believe ICE would be any more forthcoming in sharing its own footage the next time its agents gun down an innocent person on the streets of the United States of America.
