Mississippi elects first Black senator since Reconstruction [Link]

Scott Colom, 2026. Image by The Contrarian from Wikimedia Commons. Licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license.

In 1870, Mississippi made history by electing the first Black man to serve in the United States Senate. Hiram Revels was elected to finish the term of one of the state's Senate seats that had been vacant since the Civil War began. He served to the end of his term in 1871. Just a few years later, Mississippi elected another Black Senator, Blanche Bruce, who served from 1875 to 1881. That's the last time a Black person has represented Mississippi in the Senate. This happened during a period known as Reconstruction, when the newly-freed slaves flourished for a brief period, finding remarkable success in everything they did, from business to education to religion to politics. But this period was short-lived as white-supremacist groups like the Ku Klux Klan turned to violence and intimidation to prevent Black people from finding success. The establishment of Jim Crowe laws near the end of the 1800s further disenfranchised Black voters and legalized segregation and discrimination for over a century before such laws were ruled unconstitutional during the Civil Rights era. Segregation and discrimination may no longer be technically legal, but they remain in full practice throughout our country, especially in southern states like Mississippi.

This year, for the first time in 145 years, Mississippi elected a Black legislator to the United States Senate. Scott Colom will officially join the 120th U.S. Congress on January 3, 2027, and will serve for the next six years representing his state in Congress. In a remarkable election, this may be the most remarkable result of all. Congratulations to Mississippi on (once again) making history!

Trump regime refuses to participate in U.N. human rights review [Link]

United Nations Human Rights Council Logo.

Since 2006, the United Nations has routinely reviewed the human rights records of its member nations every few years to ensure that member nations are following the commitments and obligations that come with membership in the U.N. This year, the United States became the first government to refuse to participate in this review. The U.N. has extended the deadline to January to allow the U.S.A. to reconsider that decision and follow through with the review. This is the second time the council has delayed this particular review after the United States refused to participate.

Last year, the Trump regime pulled the United States out of the Human Rights Council that conducts this review, claiming that the council has an anti-U.S. bias. But the more likely reason is that the United States has been engaging in behavior that violates the human rights agreements the council will review. In particular, the aggressive deportation practices and the extrajudicial killings in the Caribbean and Pacific ocean, as well as anti-LGBT policies, racial discrimination, and the war in Iran are likely to be scrutinized.

Trump regime will make it harder to get SNAP food assistance [Link]

Image by Gerd Altmann from Pixabay.

The Trump regime is making plans to make it harder for needy people to get assistance buying food for their families. Currently, some people who receive Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) benefits automatically qualify for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP).

Under the regime's new rules, more than 6 million people may lose their SNAP benefits over the next few years. That doesn't count the 5.5 million people who have already lost SNAP benefits under the regime's relentless persecution of the poorest Americans over the past year. Being on SNAP automatically qualifies children for free meals at school, and this change will cause 1.8 million children to lose that eligibility.

The administration here is basically proposing to eliminate the flexibility that Congress gave the states to serve working poor people.

MAHA's fear-mongering leads to increase in infant mortality [Link]

Image by Shafin Al Asad Protic from Pixabay.

Vitamin K deficiency bleeding is a rare but serious condition that can cause catastrophic and often fatal bleeding in newborns. The good news is that it has been virtually eliminated in the United States since the 1960s thanks to the routine practice of giving newborns a vitamin K shot.

But the disease is seeing a comeback thanks to Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s idiotic MAHA movement that is pushing false narratives that are causing followers to distrust science and refuse to allow their children to be given the vitamin shot. The crackpot notions pushed by MAHA have led to an increase in vitamin K deficiency bleeding among newborns of 347% since 2017.
